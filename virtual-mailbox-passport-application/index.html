Using a virtual mailbox address on a passport application

Short answer: The passport forms treat two addresses differently. The mailing address (item 8) is where the State Department sends your passport. It can be a street address or a P.O. Box, and the form expects that you may not live there. The permanent address is for where you actually live: "Do not list a P.O. Box." So a mailbox address can go in item 8, but your home goes in the permanent-address box. The forms don't mention private mailboxes (PMBs) either way. To be certain, ask the National Passport Information Center (NPIC) at 1-877-487-2778.

Below is the exact wording from the current DS-11 (first passport, in person) and DS-82 (renewal by mail), both the 04-2025 editions, plus the USPS summary of the online renewal rules. All read November 28, 2026.

Renewing online

USPS lists the State Department's online renewal requirements. One of them: "You have a mailing address in a U.S. state or territory. You cannot renew online using an Army, Fleet, or Diplomatic Post Office address" [USPS]. Online renewal is routine service only, and if you need expedited service you must renew by mail [USPS]. The rule names APO, FPO and DPO addresses as the exclusions. It doesn't mention commercial mailbox addresses.

If you live abroad

The DS-82 says "U.S. citizens residing outside the United States and Canada cannot submit this form to the domestic addresses listed above" and must follow their U.S. embassy or consulate's procedures [DS-82]. The DS-11 says that in most countries you must apply in person at an embassy or consulate [DS-11]. A U.S. mailbox doesn't change where you apply from. For setting up a mailbox from overseas, see getting a U.S. virtual mailbox while living abroad.

Expect two or three envelopes

Both forms say: "You may receive your newly issued U.S. passport book and/or card and your citizenship evidence in two separate mailings. If you are applying for both a book and card, you may receive three separate mailings" [DS-11] [DS-82]. Citizenship evidence can mean your original birth certificate. At a mailbox, each envelope shows up as its own item.

Receiving a passport at your mailbox safely

  1. Use the exact mailbox address, including the box or suite number, and the name on your Form 1583. See how to write a virtual mailbox address.
  2. Turn off automatic open-and-scan if your provider has it on, so the envelope is forwarded unopened. At Traveling Mailbox, automatic scanning "tells our team to open and scan incoming letters covered by that setting" [source].
  3. Forward it with tracking, or pick it up. Ship it as soon as it arrives, or collect it if your location allows pickup (pickup rules by provider).
  4. Watch the auto-shred clock. At Traveling Mailbox and Stable, paper you don't act on is shredded after 30 days [TMB] [Stable]. See shredding policies.

US Global Mail says it logs, tracks and verifies "every single item" with a barcoding system: "Whether you're receiving a check or a passport, it's handled with care and documented at every stage" [source].
