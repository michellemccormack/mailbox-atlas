HIPAA-compliant virtual mailboxes

Short answer: If a mail service will open and scan mail that contains patient information, you'll want it to sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA). Four providers publish one: Virtual Post Mail on its Business plan ($60/mo) and up, PostScan Mail as a $25/mo add-on (billed annually) plus $0.50 per item, Stable on request (listed on its Custom plan), and US Global Mail on its Enterprise plan only.

"HIPAA compliant" on a pricing page and a signed BAA aren't the same thing. Below is what each provider actually offers, which plan you need, and what it costs, from each provider's own pages on November 24, 2026. This is a price and feature reference, not legal advice; your own compliance review still applies.

Why does a virtual mailbox need a BAA when USPS doesn't?

HHS says covered entities don't need business associate contracts with the U.S. Postal Service or private couriers that act "merely as conduits." A conduit "transports information but does not access it other than on a random or infrequent basis" [HHS]. A virtual mailbox that opens your envelopes and uploads the contents does more than carry them. That's why the providers that market to healthcare offer a BAA, and why Stable's FAQ says a provider "should be willing to sign a Business Associate Agreement" [source].

What does each one include?

Virtual Post Mail

  • Says it "will also sign a BAA to satisfy HIPAA requirements" and that mail is processed in-house "by HIPAA and SOC 2-trained staff—never by a third party" [source].
  • Unwanted mail is shredded on site, and destruction certificates are available on request [source].
  • Claims to be the only provider offering a BAA "at every location"; the BAA is available "only on Business Plans or higher" [source].
  • The Business plan includes unlimited envelope scans, 100 content scans, 10 users and 10 recipients [source]. Full plan table: Virtual Post Mail pricing.

PostScan Mail

  • HIPAA is a paid add-on: "$25.00 per month, billed annually, then $0.50 per received mail item" [source].
  • That per-item charge stacks on the plan. The Starter plan is from $10/mo with 30 items, and PostScan counts junk mail toward your limit [source] [source].
  • More fees: PostScan Mail pricing.

Stable

  • Says it is "fully HIPAA-compliant and BAAs are available," with mail data "encrypted in transit and at rest" and every action logged [source].
  • Signs a BAA "on request for healthcare clients" [source]. Its pricing page lists "HIPAA BAA" among the Custom plan's features [source]; the self-serve plans are Grow ($49/mo billed yearly) and Scale ($83/mo billed yearly) [source].
  • Stable says you can keep your current address and forward mail to its processing centers instead of taking a new address [source].

US Global Mail

  • Describes itself as "SOC 2 Type II audited and HIPAA compliant" [source].
  • The BAA is offered "on our custom plans" [source]. Its healthcare page says "HIPAA support and the Business Associate Agreement are part of our Enterprise offering" and that the team reviews your scope before you send PHI [source].
  • In the plan comparison, "HIPAA with BAA" is checked only for Enterprise. Occasional ($25 a month, or $21 billed annually), Scale ($65 or $49) and Teams ($129 or $99) don't include it [source].

What does it cost at 100 pieces of mail a month?

Example: a small practice getting 100 pieces a month, using only published prices.

  • Virtual Post Mail Business: $60/mo. Incoming envelopes are unlimited on Business, and 100 content scans are included [source].
  • PostScan Mail: the HIPAA add-on alone is $25 + 100 × $0.50 = $75/mo, before the mailbox plan. The Premium plan covers 120 items and starts at $30/mo, so the total starts around $105/mo [source].
  • Stable and US Global Mail: BAA plans are quoted individually [source] [source].

Before you sign up

  • Get the BAA signed before the first piece of PHI arrives. US Global Mail says to review the agreement and covered services "before US Global Mail receives or processes PHI" [source].
  • A BAA covers the vendor, not your whole workflow. US Global Mail notes your organization "must also assess its own policies, permitted uses, and connected systems" [source].
  • Every person and business name that gets mail still needs its own USPS Form 1583. See Form 1583 for a business.
