Form 1583 for a business

Short answer: Check "Business/Organization Use," fill in section 7 (business name, type, street address, phone and place of registration), and have a company officer sign with their title. The business doesn't need its own ID: the officer's photo ID and address ID cover it. Every business name on the mailbox needs its own Form 1583.

USPS Form 1583 works the same for a company as for a person, with a few extra boxes. Here's what the current form (June 2024 edition) and the USPS rules say, and how providers handle business names, checked November 24, 2026. For notary options and the basic ID list, see Form 1583 and online notarization.

Who signs Form 1583 for a business?

An officer. The form's instructions say: "for Business/Organization Use, an officer must sign the application and provide his or her title" [PS 1583]. Like any applicant, the officer signs in front of the mail center's staff (in person or on a live video call) or a U.S.-commissioned notary [PS 1583].

That's why your personal name appears on a company's form. iPostal1 explains that "the USPS requires a responsible party on each mailbox," and that your name is kept confidential to the mail center and USPS [source]. US Global Mail requires "1 primary name" on every business account, an officer of the company who signs Form 1583 [source].

Which IDs does a business need?

The officer's. The business itself provides none:

  • Two IDs for the applicant: a government-issued photo ID and a second document showing the address on the form [PS 1583].
  • US Global Mail: "Business names don't require their own IDs but the company officer must be on the account with two IDs" [source]. It also lists a corporate identification card as not acceptable [source].
  • One driver's license can't count as both the photo ID and the address ID [PS 1583].

What goes in section 7?

If you check "Business/Organization Use" in item 3, you must complete item 7 [PS 1583]:

FieldWhat to enter
7a Name of businessThe name mail will be addressed to
7b Type of businessWhat the business does
7c–7g Business street addressThe business's own address. Anytime Mailbox notes this "cannot be the mailbox address you are currently signing up for" [source]
7h TelephoneThe business phone
7i Place of registration"The county and state (if domestic), or the country (if foreign)" [PS 1583]
  • Not registered yet? Anytime Mailbox says to enter N/A for place of registration if the business is new or not yet registered [source]. Earth Class Mail has a "Check if not applicable" box for cases like a sole proprietorship [source].
  • Verification: USPS may check that you conduct business at the address in 7c [PS 1583].

Employees and partners who get mail (item 12)

For a business mailbox, item 12 lists the members who will receive mail there. Each person listed must present two forms of valid ID to USPS on request [PS 1583]. That's different from personal use, where each adult files a separate Form 1583 [PS 1583].

Providers handle this in different ways. US Global Mail separates recipients (people whose mail it receives; each adult needs their own two IDs and Form 1583) from users (people who log in to manage mail; no form or ID needed) [source].

When do you need a new form?

  • When information on the form changes. The applicant "must file an updated application" [PS 1583], for example when the officer changes or the business moves.
  • When an ID expires. IDs must be current, and mail centers certify every quarter that no ID on file is expired [DMM 508.1.8]. Anytime Mailbox gives an expired ID as an example of when you'll need a new form [source].
  • For each new location. iPostal1 requires a new Form 1583 for each mailbox location [source].

If yours was sent back, see Form 1583 rejected: reasons and fixes.

Where can you use the business address?

A mailbox address works as a company's mailing address, but Texas, Florida and California require a registered agent at a real in-state street address for the registered office. See virtual mailbox for an LLC and virtual mailbox for a bank account. If you're switching an existing business, the IRS change form is 8822-B [IRS]; see changing your address to a virtual mailbox.
