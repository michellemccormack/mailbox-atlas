How to cancel a virtual mailbox

Short answer: Update your senders first, because you can't file a USPS change of address away from a virtual mailbox. Download the scans you want to keep and forward or shred the paper you still have stored. Then cancel from your account before the renewal date. Mail will keep arriving for months. Depending on the provider, it gets held, forwarded for a fee, shredded or returned to the sender.

Closing a virtual mailbox is easy. Getting your mail to follow you is the hard part. Below are the USPS rule behind that and each provider's own cancellation policy, checked November 25, 2026.

The USPS rule: no change of address, 6 months of mail

  • When you leave a commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA, the USPS term for a virtual mailbox company), "neither the addressee nor the CMRA will file a change-of-address order with the Post Office" [DMM 508.1.8].
  • The CMRA "must remail mail intended for the addressee (customer) for at least 6 months after the termination date," with new postage, to the remail address on your Form 1583 [DMM 508.1.8].
  • That obligation doesn't apply if you give the CMRA written instructions not to remail, and those instructions can be in its service agreement [DMM 508.1.8]. Many providers' terms do exactly that, which is why policies differ below.
  • A CMRA may not refuse mail for a former customer within the past 6 months. After the 6 months, it returns First-Class Mail, Priority Mail and similar classes to the Post Office endorsed "Undeliverable, Commercial Mail Receiving Agency, No Authorization to Receive Mail for this Addressee" [DMM 508.1.8].

If you filed a USPS change of address to the mailbox when you signed up, that's a separate request. A temporary one ends on its own date, and you can cancel or edit it with the confirmation code USPS sent you [USGM] [Traveling Mailbox]. More on that in changing your address to a virtual mailbox.

Refunds when you cancel

  • US Global Mail: 30-day money-back guarantee; after 30 days, no refund for unused time [source].
  • PhysicalAddress.com: 30-day money-back guarantee [source].
  • Stable: 30-day money-back guarantee on your first invoice only. Add-ons like check deposits and mail forwarding aren't covered [source].
  • iPostal1: prepaid yearly plans are non-refundable after the first 30 days, and unused scan or shred bundles are non-refundable [source].
  • Anytime Mailbox: a full refund only within 30 days of sign-up and only if the mailbox was never used to receive mail [source].
  • Traveling Mailbox: annual plans are "refundable on a monthly schedule" [source].
  • Virtual Post Mail: remaining account credits are refunded [source].

Checklist before you close

  1. Get the new address working first. Traveling Mailbox suggests waiting until mail is actually arriving at the new place [source].
  2. Tell senders directly, starting with banks, the IRS and state agencies. The IRS uses Form 8822 for individuals and 8822-B for businesses [8822] [8822-B]. Virtual Post Mail recommends starting at least a month before you close [source].
  3. Cancel any USPS forwarding that points to the mailbox, as Stable recommends [source].
  4. Download your scans. At Virtual Post Mail you can't log in after termination [source].
  5. Forward or shred the paper you still have there. PostScan discards it and US Global Mail asks you to handle every item first [PostScan] [USGM].
  6. Cancel before the renewal date. US Global Mail and iPostal1 renew automatically [USGM] [iPostal1].
  7. If you need late mail, choose a provider option that covers it: US Global Mail's Wind-down Plan, Earth Class Mail's 6-month access, or Traveling Mailbox's six months of residual-mail forwarding [USGM] [ECM] [TMB].
