Switching virtual mailbox locations, or when yours closes

Short answer: At most providers you can't just edit your address. Each address has its own Form 1583, so switching usually means opening a new mailbox at the new location, completing a new form, updating your senders yourself, and keeping the old box open until mail stops arriving. USPS rules don't allow a change-of-address order when a mailbox relationship ends, so there's no automatic forwarding from the old box.

This applies when you move to a closer location, want a different state, or your provider moves a location. Each provider's own policy, checked November 28, 2026.

Why you can't file a change of address

USPS rules for mailbox companies (called CMRAs) say: "When the agency relationship between the CMRA and the addressee terminates, neither the addressee nor the CMRA will file a change-of-address order with the Post Office." The CMRA must instead remail your mail "for at least 6 months after the termination date", with new postage [DMM 508.1.8]. Virtual Post Mail says it plainly: "USPS will not allow you to file a Change of Address from one of our mailbox locations to another" [source]. Anytime Mailbox: "a change of address form cannot be submitted for a digital mailbox" [source].

Your old mail and scans don't move

  • Traveling Mailbox: "Your mail and scans stay with your previous account." Download the scans you want and forward the originals you need before closing. After closure, it receives leftover mail for up to six months, and you can still sign in to request forwarding (shipping and handling charges apply) [source].
  • US Global Mail: mail that arrives after you close the old account "will be securely shredded" [source].
  • Virtual Post Mail: the new account needs a new username, but you can transfer the mailbox to your other login in Mailbox Settings [source].

Closing the old box follows each provider's normal cancellation rules. See canceling a virtual mailbox.

When the provider closes or moves your location

Virtual Post Mail has published its own example. When it left its Walnut, California office, it gave customers until March 31, 2023, "about 6 months", to move to a new Covina address. The mailbox number stayed the same, staff processed mail at both addresses until the lease ended, and VPM said it now owns all the buildings its offices are in [source]. Its disaster plan says that if a single site has to shut for days, letters may be sent to other locations for scanning (about 3 to 5 business days of delay), while packages stay on site until it reopens [source].

US Global Mail says its own facilities are in California, New York, Las Vegas and Texas. Elsewhere, physical mail is handled at "the designated location or a Channel Partner" [source]. At Anytime Mailbox and iPostal1, mail is handled by local businesses: Anytime calls them "operator mailing partners" [Anytime], and iPostal1 says most of its locations are "professional retail pack and ship stores or business coworking centers" [iPostal1]. Neither page says what happens if a partner location closes, so ask before you sign up.

A switch checklist

  1. Open the new mailbox first and get its Form 1583 approved [TMB] [USGM].
  2. Update senders directly: banks, insurers, the IRS, your state, subscriptions. Traveling Mailbox's list: "your bank, insurers, subscriptions and personal contacts" [source]. For the IRS and other senders, see changing your address.
  3. Overlap for a month or two. VPM suggests 1 to 2 months [source] [VPM switching guide].
  4. Download scans and forward originals before closing [source].
  5. If the address is on a state filing (an LLC's principal office or mailing address), update it with the state too. See virtual mailbox for an LLC.
