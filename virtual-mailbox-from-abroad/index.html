Getting a U.S. virtual mailbox while living abroad

You can open a U.S. virtual mailbox from another country, but the USPS Form 1583 step works differently for you. Your ID, your proof of address and the person who witnesses your signature all have to meet USPS rules, and each provider adds its own. Here's what USPS says and what seven providers require, checked November 21, 2026.

What USPS requires, wherever you live

  • Who can witness your signature: the mail center (CMRA) owner, manager or an authorized employee, in person or "in real-time audio and video," or "a notary public commissioned in a United States state, territory, possession, or the District of Columbia" [DMM 508.1.8.3]. A notary commissioned only in another country isn't on that list, which is why providers steer overseas customers to a video call or a U.S. online notary.
  • Photo ID: a passport counts, and USPS defines it as "a U.S. passport, U.S. passport card, or foreign passport." A Mexican Matricula Consular card is also listed [DMM 608.10.3].
  • Address ID: a lease, mortgage or deed of trust; a voter or vehicle registration card; a home or vehicle insurance policy; Form I-94; or a second acceptable photo ID [DMM 608.10.4].
  • Not accepted: Social Security cards, birth certificates and credit cards [DMM 608.10.5].
  • Your address must match. The mail center has to confirm you live at the permanent address on the form, and "if information on the application does not match the identification, the CMRA must deny the application" [DMM 508.1.8.3]. The form asks for a country, so a foreign home address is expected [PS 1583].

The address-proof problem, and how people solve it

Your passport is usually the easy part. The second document is harder, because it has to show the home address you put on the form [DMM]. Here's what the rules allow:

  • A foreign lease or home insurance policy fits the USPS list [DMM 608.10.4]. Check language rules first: Anytime Mailbox and PostScan want English (or an English translation at PostScan) [source] [PostScan], and Earth Class Mail wants the Latin alphabet [source].
  • A second photo ID can serve as the address ID under USPS rules [DMM 608.10.4]. Whether a given provider accepts, for example, a second passport in place of a lease is up to that provider provider-specific.
  • Form I-94 is on the USPS list of address IDs [DMM 608.10.4]. Not every provider's own list includes it (Earth Class Mail's non-SSN list doesn't, for example) [source].
  • A national ID card isn't on the USPS photo-ID list, and PostScan explicitly rejects it [DMM] [PostScan].
