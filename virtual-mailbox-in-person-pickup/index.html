Picking up mail in person from a virtual mailbox

Short answer: Only at some providers. Mailboxes run through local mail centers, like Anytime Mailbox, iPostal1 and PostScan Mail, usually let you collect mail during business hours, sometimes for a small fee. PhysicalAddress.com includes free local pickup at each of its company-run locations. US Global Mail allows scheduled pickups at most of its locations for a fee. Stable and Virtual Post Mail don't offer pickup at all, so you have them ship your mail instead.

USPS rules allow either approach: a CMRA (the postal term for a virtual mailbox company) "accepts delivery of the mail and holds it for pickup or remails it to the addressee" [DMM 508.1.8]. Whether you can walk in depends on the company. Here's each provider's own policy, checked November 25, 2026.

Why some charge for pickup

iPostal1 explains it: "Since digital mailboxes are not self-service like physical mailboxes, some mail centers charge a nominal fee for processing and retrieving your mail when you come in to pick it up" [source]. Staff have to find your items and hand them over, unlike a PO Box you open with a key.

What it costs: one visit at US Global Mail

  • A stack of letters: $5 flat [source].
  • A 4 lb package: $15 + 3 × $1 = $18 [source].

For comparison, having letters shipped costs postage plus a handling fee at most providers. See mail forwarding fees compared.

Picking a mailbox for pickup

  1. Choose a location you can reach during its business hours. Pickup happens at the specific address you sign up for [iPostal1] [Anytime].
  2. Check the location's pickup fee before signing up. At Anytime Mailbox it's in each plan's Full Details; at iPostal1, on the locations page [Anytime] [iPostal1].
  3. Bring photo ID, and if someone else is collecting, add them to the account or notify the provider first [USGM] [iPostal1].
  4. Watch the storage clock. Mail you plan to collect still counts against the free storage period; see how long mail is kept.
