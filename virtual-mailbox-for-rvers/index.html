Mail for full-time RVers and travelers

Once you've sold the house, mail needs a fixed address to land at and a way to reach you on the road. There are three real options: a virtual mailbox that forwards in batches, USPS Premium Forwarding, and USPS General Delivery. Here's what each costs and where each falls short, from USPS and provider pages checked November 21, 2026.

Using a mailbox address as your legal residence: South Dakota's rules

Many full-timers pick a "domicile" state and use a mail-forwarding address there. Each state sets its own rules for driver's licenses. South Dakota publishes its rules for full-time travelers. Its Department of Public Safety requires:

  • A completed Residency Affidavit, signed in front of a notary or a South Dakota driver license examiner [SD affidavit]
  • A receipt for one night's stay at a South Dakota hotel, motel, campground or RV park within the last year [SD DPS]
  • Your South Dakota personal mailbox contract, plus one document no more than a year old showing your PMB address (a receipt from the PMB business or a piece of mail) [SD DPS]

Two lines in the affidavit matter when you choose a provider. You swear that "My personal mailbox service (PMB) is a mail forwarding service, and not a virtual only mail service," and that you don't "stay, live in, or maintain a residence in any another state" [SD affidavit]. The manual adds: "if you maintain a residence in another state, you do not qualify as a full-time traveler" [SD DPS]. The affidavit also warns that a South Dakota license or ID means you'll be called for jury duty in South Dakota [SD affidavit].

South Dakota's forms don't define "virtual only mail service." Earth Class Mail and Stable both list a Sioux Falls address [LZ] [Stable], but whether either counts as a "mail forwarding service" for the affidavit is something to confirm with SD DPS (DPSLicensingInfo@state.sd.us, 605-773-6883, the contacts listed in the manual) before you rely on it [SD DPS]. A driver's license is also only one part of domicile. Taxes, voting and insurance each have their own rules, which we don't cover here.

Other states: iPostal1 says customers have used its addresses "for their DMV needs" but that "the laws vary by state and change periodically" [source]. Check your own state's DMV before you sign up.

A setup that works for most full-timers

  1. Pick a provider with a per-shipment forwarding fee (not per item) if you forward in batches: Traveling Mailbox ($2 per box) [source], or US Global Mail or Stable (postage only) [source] [Stable].
  2. Sign Form 1583 on a video call if you can. Traveling Mailbox, Stable and US Global Mail don't need a notary. See Form 1583 and online notarization.
  3. File a USPS change of address or update your senders yourself. Form 1583 doesn't move your mail [source].
  4. Have mail sent to a campground or a Post Office General Delivery window in a town you'll be in for a while. Call the destination Post Office first to ask which facility and ZIP Code to use [DMM 508.6.3].
