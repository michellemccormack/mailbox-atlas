Virtual mailbox addresses on Stripe and Amazon seller accounts

Short answer: Not as your verified business address. Stripe's U.S. account rules ask for "the address of the physical location where the majority of your business activity is carried out," and its address-change page says "PO Boxes, HCs, and Private Mailboxes will not be accepted" [Stripe] [Stripe]. A virtual mailbox address is a private mailbox in USPS terms [DMM 508.1.8]. Amazon asks for the registered business address on your business license plus proof of your residential address [Amazon]. Use the mailbox for mail, not for these verification fields.

People often get a virtual mailbox so their home address stays off a payment or marketplace account, then hit a verification wall. Here's what Stripe and Amazon publish about addresses, and where a mailbox address can still help. Checked November 27, 2026. This is a summary of the companies' own help pages, not legal advice, and each company makes its own review decisions.

What happens if Stripe flags the address

  • Payments keep working. Accounts with a mailbox or registered agent address lose Issuing, Treasury and Opal, but "will still be able to use Stripe for payment processing and other products" [source].
  • Existing money-management accounts get 30 days. Stripe notifies them and gives "a 30-day grace period," then disables those capabilities until a valid address is added [source].
  • The error says so plainly: "The address you provided appears to be associated with a mailbox or virtual address service, which we can't accept" [source].
  • To appeal, Stripe says to update to "a physical location where you operate," or, if the address really is your location, submit a proof of address document [source]. For business-address checks it accepts a signed lease, utility bills, federal or state tax returns, or Articles of Incorporation showing the business or owner's name [source].

Amazon seller registration

Amazon's registration guide asks for two different addresses [source]:

  • Registered business address: "Enter the address that appears on your business license." [source]
  • Proof of residential address: a document "dated from the last 180 days, like a bank or credit card statement," uploaded with a government-issued ID, followed by a photo check or a video call where you bring the ID and proof of residential address [source].

A mailbox address isn't a residence, so it can't fill the second field. Whether it can be the first depends on what's on your business license, and an LLC's state filing often needs a street address for the registered office anyway; see virtual mailbox for an LLC. Amazon's guide says identity verification "usually takes three business days or less" [source].

What the mailbox providers say

  • US Global Mail: most banks and card issuers accept its address, but "some banks have recently started asking for a personal address, which is not a CMRA" address; customers then give a personal address and use the USGM address for mail [source].
  • PostScan Mail: its pricing page warns, "A mail center is not ideal for use with financial institutions and/or business registration" [source].
  • Virtual Post Mail: its pricing page says "The mailbox address is not a physical address" and points to TruLease, a leased office address it calls "ideal for satisfying compliance requirements for bank accounts, Amazon Store, and more" [source] [TruLease]. Costs are on our VPM pricing page. Whether Stripe or Amazon accepts a particular leased address is their call, not VPM's.

Where a virtual mailbox still fits

  1. Mailing address on your accounts and invoices. Keep the physical address in the verification field and route paper mail (1099s, notices, checks) to the mailbox.
  2. Formation and IRS mail. A mailbox can go on IRS Form SS-4's mailing address line [source]. See virtual mailbox for a bank account for which bank fields take a PMB.
