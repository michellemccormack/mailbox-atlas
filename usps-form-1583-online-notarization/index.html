USPS Form 1583 and online notarization

Short answer: No, USPS doesn't require a notary. Since May 1, 2024, you can sign Form 1583 on a live video call with the mail center's staff or before a U.S.-commissioned notary. Traveling Mailbox, Stable and US Global Mail verify you without a notary; iPostal1's online notary costs $25 and PostScan Mail's partner notary $20. [source][source][source][source]

Form 1583, "Application for Delivery of Mail Through Agent," is the form that lets a virtual mailbox company receive your mail. Whether you need a notary depends on the provider, not on USPS.

Does USPS require a notary for Form 1583?

A USPS rule change took effect on May 1, 2024. The applicant must sign in one of two ways:

"sign or confirm his or her signature in the physical or virtual (in real-time audio and video) presence of the CMRA owner or manager or authorized employee, or acknowledge his or her signature in the physical or virtual (in real-time audio and video) presence of a notary public commissioned in a United States state, territory, possession, or the District of Columbia." [USPS Postal Bulletin]

So a live video call with the mail center's own staff is enough under USPS rules. A notary is one option, not a requirement, and if you do use one, the notary must hold a U.S. commission [USPS]. The current form says the same thing [PS 1583].

Which IDs do you need for Form 1583?

You need two IDs: one with your photo and one showing your current address [source]. PostScan Mail's list is typical:

  • Photo ID: U.S. driver's license or state ID, passport or passport card, permanent resident card, certificate of naturalization, U.S. military or university ID, and a few others [source]
  • Address ID: driver's license (if you didn't use it as your photo ID), voter or vehicle registration, vehicle or home insurance policy, lease, mortgage or deed of trust [source]

Not accepted: Social Security cards, credit cards and birth certificates [source]. One license can't count as both IDs [source].

Things that trip people up

  • Form 1583 doesn't forward your mail. It only authorizes the mail center. You still have to file a change of address or update your senders [source].
  • Every name needs its own form. Traveling Mailbox requires a form and two IDs "for each name that mail will be received under" [source]. PostScan charges $5 a month per extra recipient [source].
  • Switching locations means a new form. Traveling Mailbox requires a new Form 1583 for every recipient when you move to a different location [source].

An independent online notary

If your provider requires a notarized form, some send you to their own partner and others let you bring a form that was notarized separately. Confirm which yours accepts before you pay.

BlueNotary is an online notary that says it can notarize Form 1583 over a live video session: you upload the form and an ID, then meet a commissioned online notary. It says a notary acknowledgment can take less than 10 minutes [source]. Its fee schedule lists on-demand notarization at $25 for one stamp [source].

Notarize, which now operates under the Proof name, also offers online notarization of Form 1583: you upload the form, verify your identity, and meet a notary on a live video call. Its Form 1583 page lists the first notary seal at $25 and says signers complete their notarization in less than 10 minutes on average, with notaries available 24/7 [source].
