Virtual mailbox pricing, compared

The sticker price is the smallest part of the bill. What decides the real cost is how many pieces of mail are included, what each scan past that costs, and what forwarding costs. Here are the published numbers for eight services, copied from each provider's own page on November 19, 2026.

How to read this: "from" prices vary by location for iPostal1, Anytime Mailbox and PostScan Mail, because independent mail centers set them. The table shows the lowest price each provider advertises. Annual billing is cheaper at several providers, and both prices are shown where the page lists both.

What a month really costs: one example

Say you get 30 letters a month and want 10 of them opened and scanned (about 3 pages each), with nothing forwarded.

  • Traveling Mailbox Basic: $15. 30 envelopes and 30 page scans fit inside the 40-envelope / 35-page allowance [source].
  • PostScan Mail Starter: from $10, plus $2.00 for each of the 10 open-and-scans ($20), so from $30. The Starter plan lists no free open-and-scans; Standard includes 5 [source].
  • Stable Grow: $59 monthly ($49 if paid yearly). 30 pieces is the monthly limit, and open-and-scan is free [source].
  • Earth Class Mail Starter: $39 covers 5 digitized items, so 30 items is well past the allowance. The overage rate wasn't clear in our capture unverified.

Someone who scans very little and mostly wants an address does best with a cheap Anytime Mailbox or iPostal1 location. Anyone who wants most of their mail opened pays less with a plan that includes page scans.

Street address vs P.O. Box

Every provider above gives you a street address at a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA), not a USPS P.O. Box. USPS requires every CMRA customer to sign Form 1583 before the CMRA can handle their mail [USPS]. Some banks and agencies won't accept a CMRA address. Anytime Mailbox, for example, says it doesn't recommend using its address as your official business address because "some states and institutions may have restrictions" [source].
