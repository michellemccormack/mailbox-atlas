Does junk mail count toward your virtual mailbox limit?

Short answer: At most providers, no. Traveling Mailbox, Stable, LegalZoom Virtual Mail and Virtual Post Mail exclude junk from your monthly limit, most iPostal1 locations discard it without counting it, and US Global Mail has no mail-piece limit. PostScan Mail is the exception: junk counts, and every item over your plan costs $0.50.

Most plans cap the pieces of mail you can receive each month, and catalogs and credit card offers can eat that cap fast. Here's how each provider treats junk, and what an extra piece costs once you're over, from each provider's own pages on November 24, 2026.

The fine print

  • Traveling Mailbox: "anything you mark as 'Junk' is shredded for free and does not count towards your plan limits" [source]. So it's your click that keeps it off the count.
  • Virtual Post Mail: an older help article says "any mail that shows up in your online mailbox counts toward your quota." Mail addressed to "Current Resident/Occupant" is trashed automatically, but things like a bank's debit card offer get uploaded to be safe [source]. This only matters on Starter, which caps envelopes at 25 [source].
  • Stable: Grow allows 30 pieces a month (360 a year on yearly billing) and Scale 50 (600 a year). Junk "will be filtered out" before counting [source].
  • US Global Mail: doesn't limit incoming mail, but says accounts with "unusually high volumes" may be reviewed [source].
  • iPostal1: the rule applies at "most" locations, not all [source].

What does junk cost on a capped plan?

Say you get 40 pieces a month and 15 of them are junk.

  • PostScan Mail Starter (30 items, from $10/mo): all 40 count, so 10 over × $0.50 = $5 a month extra [source] [source].
  • Stable Grow (30 pieces): 25 count, so no overage [source].
  • iPostal1 $9.99 plan (30 items): if your location discards the junk, 25 count and there's no overage. If it doesn't, 10 × $0.35 = $3.50 [source] [source].
  • Traveling Mailbox Basic (40 envelopes): within the limit either way [source].

How can you cut junk at the source?

  • Prescreened credit and insurance offers: the CFPB says you can opt out at OptOutPrescreen.com or by calling 1-888-5-OPTOUT. A phone or online opt-out lasts five years; a permanent opt-out needs a signed form by mail [CFPB].
  • Don't forward advertising: a USPS change of address doesn't forward USPS Marketing Mail unless the sender has paid for forwarding [USPS]. If you move your mail to a virtual mailbox that way, most bulk advertising stays behind.
