Virtual mailbox vs PO Box

Short answer: A USPS PO Box is usually cheaper, $31 to $170 per six months for the smallest box depending on ZIP Code, but you collect mail in person and the address reads "PO Box." A virtual mailbox, from $9.99 a month at iPostal1, gives a street address with a PMB number, accepts packages and scans your mail. [source][source]

How much does a PO Box cost?

USPS prices a PO Box by box size and by the "fee group" of its ZIP Code. Areas served by a competing private mailbox provider are "Competitive" (groups C30 to C44); the rest are "Market Dominant" (groups 1 to 7) [DMM 508.4] [Notice 123]. Prices per 6-month period, effective October 4, 2026:

Fees: USPS Notice 123 price list, "PO Box Service," semi-annual tables [source]. Box dimensions: DMM 508.4.1.3 [source]. To find your ZIP Code's fee group, USPS points to its PO Box page or your local Post Office [USPS].

  • Per year, the smallest box runs $62 to $340 (two 6-month periods), depending on where you live [Notice 123].
  • New customers at Competitive locations get 13 months for a 12-month payment and pay no deposit for the first two keys [DMM 508.4.5.4].
  • Keys and locks: $6 deposit per key, $14 per extra or replacement key after the first two, $30 lock replacement or late-payment fee [Notice 123].
  • Three-month terms exist at some Post Offices if you set up automatic renewal (smallest box: $21 to $50 Market Dominant, $46 to $98 Competitive) [Notice 123] [DMM 508.4.1.7].
  • Free PO Boxes (Group E) exist only for people whose address USPS won't deliver to, under specific conditions [DMM 508.4.5.2].
  • Refunds: cancel in the first 3 months of a 6-month period and you get half back; after that, nothing [DMM 508.4.6.1].

The street-address option at some Post Offices

At Competitive locations, you can sign a customer agreement to use the Post Office's street address with your box number, written like "123 Main Street #4567." With that option you can also get packages from private carriers at the box, within USPS size and weight limits (70 lb, 130 inches length plus girth) [DMM 508.4.5.4]. If your Post Office offers it, this gives you most of what a basic virtual mailbox address does, except scanning and forwarding.

Where neither address works

  • Your LLC's registered office: Texas says a registered office "cannot solely be the address of a mailbox service" [Texas SOS]. More in virtual mailbox for an LLC.
  • The IRS physical address line: Form SS-4 tells you not to enter a P.O. box as the physical location on line 5 [IRS].
  • A bank's home or business street address: federal rules require a street address for individuals and a physical location for companies [31 CFR 1020.220], and some banks refuse PO boxes and virtual addresses in those fields [Mercury]. See can I use a virtual mailbox for my bank account?

Both work as a mailing address in most of those places, and the IRS uses your SS-4 mailing address "on all tax returns" [IRS].

Should you choose a PO Box or a virtual mailbox?

  • PO Box: you live near the Post Office, get mostly letters, and want the lowest price. Check whether your Post Office offers the street-address option if you get UPS or FedEx deliveries [DMM 508.4.5.4].
  • Virtual mailbox: you travel, live abroad, or want mail scanned and forwarded. A full-time traveler can't check a PO Box; see mail for full-time RVers and living abroad.
