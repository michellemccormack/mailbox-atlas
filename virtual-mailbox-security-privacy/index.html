Who opens your mail? Virtual mailbox security and privacy

Short answer: Staff at the mail center handle every piece and scan the outside of each envelope. They open a letter only when you ask for a content scan, or when an auto-scan setting you turned on covers it. Who those staff are depends on the model. Providers that run their own facilities, like Traveling Mailbox, Virtual Post Mail and US Global Mail's own locations, describe background-checked employees and on-site shredding. Network providers, like Anytime Mailbox, iPostal1 and US Global Mail's partner locations, have your mail handled by a local business.

Every claim below comes from the provider's own security, FAQ or help pages, read November 28, 2026. These are the providers' descriptions of themselves; we haven't audited them.

When is your mail opened?

  • Traveling Mailbox: "We scan the outside first. We open a letter when you request a scan or when your automatic scanning settings apply. You can also ask us to forward it unopened" [source].
  • iPostal1: "We open your letter mail only if you specifically request us to scan the contents." Packages are opened only for a content photo or "to ensure compliance with shipping regulations" [source].
  • Virtual Post Mail: "By default, we do not open and scan the content of mail arriving at our facility." For private contents it suggests skipping scans and having mail shipped instead [source].
  • Anytime Mailbox: you choose open and scan, shred, forward or pickup for each item [source].

Who else sees your mail data

  • Virtual Post Mail doesn't share personal information "unless we are served with proper documentation such as a subpoena or court order", and uses outside services such as AWS, CRM and email systems to run its service [source].
  • Traveling Mailbox: "We don't sell or share personal information for third-party marketing," apart from limited sharing with service providers like payment processors [source].
  • USPS requires every mailbox company to keep your Form 1583 and ID on file and in the USPS CMRA Customer Registration Database [DMM 508.1.8]. That's the law, not a provider choice. See Form 1583.

How to keep sensitive mail private

  1. Leave auto-scan off for personal mail and choose open-and-scan one item at a time [TMB].
  2. Ship sensitive items unopened. Traveling Mailbox ("Ship to Me") and VPM both suggest this [TMB] [VPM].
  3. Turn on two-factor sign-in where it's offered.
  4. Shred, don't just discard, anything with account numbers. See shredding fees and policies.
  5. For patient mail, you need a Business Associate Agreement. See HIPAA-compliant virtual mailboxes.
