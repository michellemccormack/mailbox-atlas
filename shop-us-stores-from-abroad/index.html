Shopping U.S. stores from abroad: consolidation and forwarding fees

Short answer: Have your orders delivered to a U.S. mailbox address, let several packages pile up, then ask the provider to combine them into one box and ship it to you. Consolidating usually saves postage, but the repack fee varies: Traveling Mailbox charges nothing extra (postage + $2 per shipment), iPostal1 $1.95 per package, US Global Mail $5 per box, and Virtual Post Mail $3.75 per package handling. You fill out the customs declaration, and the provider may open boxes to check what's inside.

This page is about packages, meaning things you buy. For forwarding letters overseas and getting a mailbox with a foreign ID, see getting a U.S. virtual mailbox while living abroad. Every fee below is from the provider's own page, read November 28, 2026. Postage is always extra and depends on weight, size and destination.

Worked example: four orders in one box

Four small packages arrive from four U.S. stores, and you ship them together in one box. Repack and handling fees only, with postage left out:

  • Traveling Mailbox: $2 (one shipment, consolidation free) [source]
  • US Global Mail: $5 (one consolidated box) [source]
  • iPostal1: 4 × $1.95 = $7.80 [source]
  • PostScan Mail Basic Locker: 4 × ($3 + $2) = $20, plus the $4.95 monthly locker fee [source]

Virtual Post Mail is left out because its help page lists handling as "$3.75 / package" without saying whether that's per package received or per box shipped [source]. The bigger number is usually postage. Combining four boxes into one is what lowers it, and Virtual Post Mail notes that shipping boxes unopened inside an "overpack" box "may increase the shipping cost" because of the extra weight [source].

Customs: you're the one declaring

  • You declare contents and value. Virtual Post Mail: "we are not a customs broker", and "it is your responsibility to ensure that what you ship is acceptable for the destination country" [source]. Vague descriptions like "bag" "can result in the shipment being returned by Customs" [source].
  • Europe needs more data. VPM says most European countries now require a harmonized (HS) code for each declared item [source]. It also says EU customs began requiring an HS code plus product identifiers on shipments to non-VAT-registered recipients from November 1, 2026 [source].
  • Boxes may be opened. VPM reserves the right to open packages to check for hazardous, illegal or customs-restricted items [source]. iPostal1 opens packages "to ensure compliance with shipping regulations" [source].
  • Batteries slow things down. Lithium batteries are hazardous materials to carriers. VPM pauses those shipments, and as a re-shipper it can't sign a Dangerous Goods Form for you [source].
  • Duties are on you. Anytime Mailbox warns of taxes or duty fees depending on contents [source].

Before you start ordering

  1. Pick a location that ships internationally. At iPostal1 it isn't available everywhere [source].
  2. Mind the storage clock while packages accumulate: 30 days free at Traveling Mailbox [source], 7 or 14 days on PostScan lockers [source]. See storage fees by provider.
  3. Check size limits before buying furniture or anything large. See receiving packages.
  4. Keep receipts for the declared value, and insure anything expensive.
