Best virtual mailbox for an LLC (and what it can't do)

An LLC deals with up to three addresses: a registered office where its registered agent accepts lawsuits, a principal office, and a mailing address. A virtual mailbox is built for the last one. It can't be the registered office unless someone there actually serves as your registered agent. Here's what the states and the IRS ask for, and which providers fill the gap. Checked November 20, 2026. This is a summary of published rules, not legal advice.

1. The registered office: a mailbox alone doesn't qualify

  • Texas: "An entity's registered office must be a physical address in Texas where the registered agent can be personally served with process during business hours. It cannot solely be the address of a mailbox service or telephone answering service." [Texas SOS] The Certificate of Formation instructions say the same, citing BOC § 5.201 [Form 201].
  • Florida: every LLC must keep a registered office and a registered agent "whose business address is identical to the address of the registered office" [§605.0113]. The articles must give the agent's "street address in this state" [§605.0201].
  • California: the Secretary of State's FAQ says the agent for service of process must be an individual who lives in California, or a registered "1505" corporate agent. The agent's name and "physical street address" are public record [CA SOS].

In practice, that means your registered agent is either you (at a real street address in the state, open during business hours) or a registered agent company. A mail-scanning address doesn't fill this role on its own.

2. The principal office and mailing address: where a virtual mailbox fits

  • Texas asks for an initial mailing address in the certificate of formation, which the Comptroller uses for tax mail. It "may be a post office box or street address" [Form 201].
  • Florida asks for "the street and mailing addresses of the company's principal office" [§605.0201]. Whether a commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA) address is accepted as the principal street address isn't stated in the statute not addressed in statute.
  • IRS (Form SS-4, EIN): line 4 is the mailing address, and "generally, this address will be used on all tax returns." Line 5 is the physical address, only if different, and you shouldn't enter a P.O. box there [IRS]. A virtual mailbox address works as a mailing address on line 4.
State rules vary. US Global Mail says a virtual or PMB address "can be used to open an LLC but there are some states that do not allow it," and names Washington and California as requiring physical addresses [USGM]. We haven't confirmed that against those states' filing rules provider claim, not state-confirmed. iPostal1 likewise says "laws vary by state" [iPostal1 FAQ]. Check your state's filing instructions before you file.

3. Banks

US Global Mail says most banks accept its address. It adds that "some banks have recently started asking for a personal address, which is not a CMRA" address [USGM]. We found no published bank-by-bank rules bank policies not published, so ask your bank before you apply.
