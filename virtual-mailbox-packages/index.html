Receiving packages at a virtual mailbox

Short answer: Yes. Because a virtual mailbox is a street address, it takes deliveries from UPS, FedEx, DHL and Amazon as well as USPS. US Global Mail and PostScan Mail name Amazon specifically [USGM] [PostScan]. What varies is how long packages are stored free, size limits, and, at Traveling Mailbox, a separate package address [TMB].

The practical questions are which carriers each provider accepts, whether big boxes are a problem, how long a package sits free, and what it costs after that. All from the providers' own pages, checked November 27, 2026. For shipping packages onward, see forwarding fees.

Why a PO Box can't do this

Virtual Post Mail puts it simply: "A regular PO box only allows you to accept parcels from USPS itself" [source]. Some Post Offices offer a street-address option for PO Boxes; see virtual mailbox vs PO Box for how that works.

Addressing a package so it isn't refused

  • Your name plus the mailbox number on every label. USGM: "be sure the sender includes your name and mailbox number on the label" [source]. Traveling Mailbox says to replace the placeholder with your real mailbox number and use an authorized recipient name [source].
  • Only names on your Form 1583. If a CMRA has no Form 1583 on file for the addressee, USPS rules say it must return the mail marked "No Authorization to Receive Mail for this Addressee" [DMM 508.1.8]. See Form 1583 for a business for adding names.
  • Check the format (PMB, # or Suite) in how to write a virtual mailbox address.

Large and heavy items

  • Traveling Mailbox: if an item "must arrive through a freight service, it is not eligible for delivery," and it asks you to check the seller's shipping method first. It doesn't repack [source].
  • PostScan Mail: accepts oversized boxes, with a $0.25 per lb surcharge over 25 lbs or 110 inches in length and girth [source].
  • iPostal1: big boxes get less free storage: 5 days at or over 24×17×12 inches, versus 30 days under 12×12×12 [source].

What a package costs to hold: one example

A 10 lb box left 10 days past the free period costs 10 × $0.10 × 10 = $10 at US Global Mail, PostScan Mail or Traveling Mailbox, which all charge $0.10 per lb per day [USGM] [PostScan] [TMB]. The difference is when that clock starts: day 4 at PostScan on a monthly plan, day 31 at Traveling Mailbox. For letters too, see how long mail is kept.
