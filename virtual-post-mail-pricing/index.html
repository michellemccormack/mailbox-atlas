Virtual Post Mail pricing

Virtual Post Mail (VPM) sells three different things: a regular virtual mailbox ($20 to $90 a month), a leased office address called TruLease, and a residential address called TruResidence. The mailbox plans are the ones most people need, and they come with a free registered agent in California, Delaware, Florida, Nevada and Texas. Here's every published price, as of November 22, 2026.

The jump from Starter to Plus pays off once you open more than about 12 letters a month, or get more than 55 pieces. For a cheaper flat plan with more envelopes included, Traveling Mailbox's $15 Basic covers 40 envelopes and 35 page scans [source] (see Traveling Mailbox pricing).

The free registered agent: what's covered

  • States: California, Nevada, Delaware, Florida and Texas [source].
  • Conditions: your mailbox address has to be in the same state as the registered agent service, the business must be registered in that state, and the business must be added as a recipient on your mailbox. Extra recipient fees apply if that puts you over your plan's free recipients [source].
  • What VPM does: receives "legal, tax, and corporate compliance documents for the company," and scans served documents the same day. "We do not handle any filings on your behalf" [source] [VPM RA].

For comparison: Stable charges $25/month per state for its registered agent add-on, and a Stable plan is required [source]. iPostal1 charges $99/year with any business plan [source]. US Global Mail says it doesn't offer registered agent service [source]. Why a mailbox by itself can't be your registered office is covered in virtual mailbox for an LLC.

TruLease and TruResidence: when a mailbox isn't enough

VPM says plainly that its mailbox address "is not a physical address," and that "a virtual mailbox won't work for banking, lease agreements, or utility bills" [source]. It sells two pricier products for those uses:

TruRes Premium+$300/mo, $3,600 billed annuallyResidential address in CA, FL, NV, SD or TX, 5 recipients; leasing is an add-on [source]

TruLease requires incorporation documents, an EIN, proof the signer is authorized, and a notarized Form 1583 [source]. TruResidence's optional lease adds a $1,200 deposit and rent that "varies by address" [source]. VPM says TruLease "does NOT guarantee outcomes" for any particular use [source].

Before paying $300 or more a month, read can I use a virtual mailbox for my bank account?: some banks only want a home address for the owner, which you may already have.

Form 1583

VPM requires "a notarized Form 1583 and two IDs" before it processes mail; documents are processed within 1 business day [source]. You only need the form for the mailbox owner and/or one company to start, but USPS can ask for forms from any listed recipient [source]. Online notary options are on our Form 1583 page.
